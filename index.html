01 · WORKSHOP SCOPE

Securing the
quantum-sensing pipeline.

Q-SENSE will address the emerging security and dependability challenges associated with quantum sensing and the practical requirements for integrating quantum sensors with existing computing, communication, embedded, and critical-infrastructure systems.

The workshop will examine security across the complete quantum-sensing pipeline, including quantum transducers, analogue and digital interfaces, control electronics, embedded processing, communication links, classical post-processing, data provenance, and AI-assisted interpretation.

A further focus will be the management of noise, uncertainty, faults, and adversarial interference throughout the sensing and post-processing pipeline. The workshop will consider how legitimate environmental disturbances, equipment faults, processing artefacts, and malicious manipulation can be distinguished and mitigated.

ALIGNMENT WITH DATE

Q-SENSE is particularly aligned with the DATE community because the deployment of trustworthy quantum sensors requires advances in secure system architecture, hardware-software co-design, embedded intelligence, test and validation, dependability, communication security, and the integration of emerging technologies with cyber-physical infrastructure.

Q
WORKSHOP OBJECTIVE

The workshop aims to identify priority research challenges, share experimental and deployment experience, facilitate access to relevant facilities and testbeds, and establish collaborative directions for secure, interoperable, dependable, and deployable quantum-sensing systems.

02 · TOPICS OF INTEREST

Research themes

Topics include, but are not limited to:

01

Security & Trust

  • Threat models and trust models for quantum sensors
  • Trustworthy-by-design quantum-sensing systems
  • Sensor integrity, authentication, attestation, and provenance
  • Physical and cyberattacks against quantum sensors
  • Sensor tampering, spoofing, manipulation, and adversarial interference
  • Secure quantum-sensor architectures
  • Zero-trust approaches to sensing infrastructure
  • Privacy risks arising from high-resolution quantum sensing
02

Architectures & Infrastructure Integration

  • Secure quantum-sensor networks
  • Secure interfaces between quantum and classical components
  • Integration with embedded, edge, and legacy systems
  • Interoperability and standards for quantum-sensing infrastructure
  • Secure communication, control, and data-acquisition channels
  • Post-quantum security for sensing infrastructure
  • Quantum sensing for security applications
03

Dependability, Test & Assurance

  • Noise-aware security and uncertainty quantification
  • Fault-tolerant and resilient quantum sensing
  • Test, validation, verification, and calibration
  • Distinguishing environmental noise, equipment faults, and malicious disturbances
  • Benchmarking and evaluation methodologies
  • Graceful degradation and recovery mechanisms
  • Assurance and certification of quantum-sensing systems
04

Data Processing & Trustworthy AI

  • Secure acquisition, transmission, storage, and processing of sensing data
  • AI-based detection of sensor attacks and anomalies
  • Explainable and trustworthy sensing analytics
  • Adversarial robustness of quantum-sensing analytics
  • Generalisation, overfitting, and distribution shift in sensing models
  • Secure and dependable AI-assisted post-processing
05

Applications, Facilities & Deployment Experience

  • Quantum sensing for critical infrastructure, navigation, communications, healthcare, manufacturing, space, defence, and scientific measurement
  • Practical experiences of accessing quantum facilities and testbeds
  • Experimental platforms, datasets, tools, and demonstrators
  • Infrastructure-integration case studies
  • Deployment challenges and lessons learned
  • Standardisation and regulatory considerations
03 · REVIEW MODEL & PROCESS

Rigorous review.
Interdisciplinary discussion.

SINGLE-BLIND peer review ≥ 2 reviewers per submission

All submitted contributions will undergo a single-blind peer-review process. Each submission will normally be evaluated by at least two members of the Technical Programme Committee.

Submissions will be assessed according to

01Relevance to the workshop scope
02Technical quality and soundness
03Novelty and significance
04Clarity of presentation
05Potential to stimulate interdisciplinary discussion
06Relevance to the DATE community

The Technical Programme Committee will include researchers and practitioners with expertise in quantum sensing, quantum technologies, hardware and systems security, embedded systems, signal processing, communications, trustworthy AI, dependability, test, and infrastructure integration.

Conflicts of interest will be identified through reviewer declarations and managed by the Programme Chairs. Organisers will not participate in decisions involving submissions for which they have a conflict of interest.

Accepted contributions will be assigned to lightning-talk, poster, or combined presentation formats according to their technical content, maturity, and suitability for interactive discussion. The final decisions will be made by the Programme Chairs on the basis of the reviews and programme balance.
